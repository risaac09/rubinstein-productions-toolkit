# Field Guide

The toolkit is organized by artifact type: methodology, prompts, CLI, templates, production. That layout serves the person who built it. If you arrived here wanting to use these practices, the better map is by role. This guide gives you three entry paths, each a reading order plus a first working session.

## Pick your path

- **[Writers](writers.md)**: narrative, copy, story development, publishing. You want the voice system, the question bank, and the editorial disciplines.
- **[Producers](producers.md)**: film, video, facilitation, client delivery. You want the session methodology, the shoot and post workflow, and the pipeline tools.
- **[Evaluators](evaluators.md)**: measurement, impact, grants. You want the Bilingual Dashboard, the coding manual, and the grant infrastructure.

The paths overlap on purpose. A facilitated documentary session produces footage for the producer, language for the writer, and coded evidence for the evaluator. Read your own path first, then skim the other two to see what your collaborators will be working from.

## Shared foundation

Two documents underlie every path. Read them before or alongside your first session.

1. **[Methodology Blueprint](../../methodology/methodology-blueprint.md)**. The single best orientation to the whole practice: the four phases (Container, Excavation, Performance, Translation), the service tiers, and how the pieces earn their keep.
2. **[Glossary](../../methodology/glossary.md)**. The term bank, with citations. When a guide says co-regulation, consent loop, or ventral vagal state, this is where the definition lives.

## Reading conventions

A few things to know before you open the source documents.

- **Canonical tier names.** The current service tiers are Founder Story, Program Engagement, and Organizational Embedding. Some older files still say Mirror, Map, and Territory, or Gateway and Mid-Tier. Those names are legacy. Where a document contradicts the [Methodology Blueprint](../../methodology/methodology-blueprint.md) on naming or pricing, the blueprint wins.
- **Wiki links.** Links written as `[[Double Brackets]]` resolve inside the author's Obsidian vault, not in this repository. Treat them as titles to search for, not links to click.
- **Sibling repos.** A few documents cite `stack-data` paths. That repo is the author's operational source of truth and is cloned as a sibling directory in his setup. If you don't have it, the citation still tells you what the claim rests on.
- **Aspirational content.** Revenue projections and expansion plans in some documents are marked speculative. Adopt the methods and skip the forecasts.

## How the pieces connect

The practice runs as a loop. Methodology defines how a session works. Prompts encode that methodology so an AI collaborator holds it during real work. Templates capture what a session produces. The CLI moves prospects and funders through a pipeline so sessions keep happening. Production turns sessions into deliverable film. Evaluation reads the whole loop and reports what changed, in two registers at once.

Take any single piece on its own if that is all you need. The [license](../../LICENSE) is MIT and the posture is take what's useful.
