# Carrying deliverable quality across models

Why document and deck quality drops when the same skills and agents are pointed at a different model, and what actually transfers.

Written 2026-08-13, from a review of six months of client deck and document production (roughly 40 PowerPoint and Word artifacts across eight version chains).

---

## The finding

Quality in this work is not carried by taste. It is carried by six mechanical disciplines, and every one of them is a procedure a model either runs or skips.

The evidence for this is uncomfortable and it comes from our own output, not from a competitor's. In one deck chain, a kicker text box was placed over an inherited body placeholder, which renders as garbled overlapping text. It shipped through **seven consecutive generations** of Claude-assisted edits. Every pass edited XML correctly. None of them re-rendered the slide and looked at it.

So the defect was not caused by a model lacking judgment about typography. It was caused by a loop step being skipped. That is the whole thesis: **when the loop runs, quality is high; when the loop is skipped, quality collapses, and it collapses on any model.**

---

## The six disciplines

Each of these is checkable, teachable, and portable. None requires the model to have good taste.

**1. Extract brand truth from the file. Never type a hex from memory.**
Observed drift in our own from-scratch builds: `#FE6D00` where the template says `#FD6D00`, `#0082A6` where the template says `#0082A5`. One bit off, every time. Across five documents in one program, five different teals were used, because everyone approximated by eye. The fix is a grep, not a judgment.

**2. Build inside the client's own file.**
Brand fidelity comes from file lineage, not from reconstruction. Duplicating their template inherits masters, footer geometry, logo images, and the tagline (which is an image, not text) for free. Rebuilding from scratch means approximating all of it. This single choice removes most of the surface where a weaker model degrades.

**3. Freeze the content before forking the form.**
The cleanest work in the corpus locked its claims in one pass, then produced two different visual treatments of that locked content twenty-six minutes apart. Fact-checking and design were separate passes. When they are one pass, the model trades accuracy for polish without telling you.

**4. Render, look, fix, re-render.**
The step that gets skipped. A model editing OOXML is composing blind unless it converts to an image and inspects it. This is also the step that most often is not *available* on another platform, which matters more than the model's ability.

**5. Rank defects before fixing them, then verify the fix against the ranking.**
In one documented review-and-fix loop, 6 of 13 prescriptions silently did not land, and one successful fix introduced a new overflow. A loop without a verify step leaves both kinds of gap invisible.

**6. Named gaps, never invented values.**
`[TK]`, `TBD, confirm with <name>`, `Sample SWOT` set in the accent color in the title. A model that fills a gap to look finished has damaged the artifact in a way that is very hard to detect downstream.

## Why the same architecture gives worse results elsewhere

Three causes, in order of how much they actually explain.

### 1. The loop has no tools to run on (largest cause)

Discipline 4 requires LibreOffice, `pdftoppm`, `markitdown`, and image inspection. If the environment cannot render a `.pptx` to an image and the model cannot look at that image, the QA loop is not being skipped by choice, it is impossible. The model ships its first draft every time.

Check this before blaming the model. Ask the tool to render a deck to JPG and describe what is on slide 3. If it cannot, no skill file will fix the output.

### 2. The skill encodes vocabulary rather than mechanism

Most skill files say things like "use clean visual hierarchy" and "make it look professional." That is a request for taste, and taste is exactly the part that does not transfer. A skill that says `grep -rho 'typeface="[^"]*"' tpl/ppt/slideLayouts | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn` transfers completely.

Audit any skill you are porting with one question: **could a competent person with no design sense follow this and get the right answer?** If not, it will degrade on a weaker model.

### 3. Genuine model difference (smallest, and last)

There is a real per-model difference in editorial and layout judgment, and it does favor Claude for this class of work. But it is the third cause, not the first, and it is the only one you cannot fix. Spending effort here before fixing causes 1 and 2 is wasted.

## What to carry to a new model

Three things, in this order.

**1. `BRAND-PROFILE.md`** for the client or brand in question. Generated by Phase 1 of `branded-deck-build` or Phase 2 of `branded-doc-build`. This is the highest-value portable artifact in the whole system, because it converts the part that requires reading files into a page of text that any model can consume. Regenerate it whenever the template changes. Keep it next to the template, not in the deck.

**2. The two skills**, `branded-deck-build.md` and `branded-doc-build.md`. Both are self-contained on purpose, with the extraction commands inlined, so neither depends on the other or on this repo being present.

**3. The template and the source material as files**, not as descriptions. A model that has the `.pptx` can extract truth from it. A model that has a description of the `.pptx` is guessing.

### What does not transfer, and what to do about it

The judgment calls: which slide deserves the deck's one inverted full-bleed treatment, whether a chart is quietly dishonest, whether a sentence is the strongest one in the document. Encode what you can as hard rules (never truncate an axis on a bounded scale; the blocking decision gets a slide of its own; name the strongest sentence in every review), then expect to supply the rest yourself on a weaker model.

The realistic expectation: porting the skills closes a large part of the gap, because most of the gap was mechanism. It does not close all of it.

---

## The fastest diagnostic

When a deliverable comes back weak, ask in this order:

1. Did it read the template file, or describe it? (Check whether it named exact hexes and the exact typeface.)
2. Did it render the output and look at it? (Ask what is on slide 5.)
3. Did it build inside the client's file, or start fresh?
4. Are there invented values where there should be named gaps?
5. Was the content settled before the form was chosen?

Four of those five are answerable by inspecting the artifact, and all five are fixable without changing models.
