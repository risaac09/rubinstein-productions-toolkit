# Your Shooting Brief

> **Status:** draft, 2026-10-16. The client-side brief for the camera-shipping model, written to the choices Isaac made on Amendment 2, item 15. No client has used it yet.

This page is written for you, the person filming. Print it and put it in the box. It should take about ten minutes to read, and it fits on two pages.

The camera arrives set up. Your job is to point it at yourself and your place, answer some questions, and send it back.

Two words you will see a lot. A **take** is one recording, from the moment you press record to the moment you stop. The **kit checklist** is the list of what is in the box. The **footage checklist** is the list further down this page, for what you film.

## When the box arrives

- **Check the box against the kit checklist.** It is in the box. Open the box on camera or on a call with us, and tick every line by the date we give you. Tell us the same day if anything is damaged or missing, with photographs. A report that comes later is harder to sort out.
- **Join the setup call.** It is short. We power the camera on, you record a test clip and play it back, and we check the picture and the sound together. If something is wrong, this is where we fix it. The date is in the box.

## What you film

1. **Your answers.** You get a list of prompts. Answer each one on camera, in your own words. There are no right answers and no need to rehearse.
2. **Your place and your work.** Film the rooms, the tools, the hands, the light through the afternoon. Hold each shot still for ten seconds or more. Editors call these shots b-roll: the pictures that play over your voice and cover the gaps between your answers. More is better than less.
3. **Room tone.** This is the sound of a room with nobody talking. In each place you film, stay quiet and film thirty seconds of nothing happening. Do not talk, and ask anyone nearby to stay quiet too. The editor uses it to make the sound smooth.

## Setting up

- **Leave the settings alone.** The camera was set before it left. Do not change the menus, the frame rate (how many pictures the camera records each second), the picture profile (the camera's colour and contrast setting) or the resolution (how sharp the picture is). If something looks wrong, call or write to us first.
- **Hold it sideways.** Film landscape, wide not tall, every time. We can make tall versions later from a wide shot. We cannot make a wide shot from a tall one.
- **Put the lens at eye level.** Set the camera on the stand so the lens is level with your eyes. You look at the lens when you answer, or just beside it, whichever feels easier.
- **Let the light fall on your face.** Face a window, never stand with one behind you. A lamp beside the lens works too. If your face is dark on the screen, turn around until it is not.
- **Clip the microphone on.** For anything spoken, the small clip-on microphone (a lav) goes on your collar or shirt, about a hand's width below your chin. Check that the cable is not rubbing on fabric.
- **One answer, one take.** Start a new take for each prompt. Stop recording between them. Short takes are easier to find later.
- **Say who it is, then clap.** Press record. Say the date, the camera name we gave you and the take number ("October 16, GH7, take three"). Then clap your hands once in front of the lens, and answer. If you forget at the start, do it at the end of the take with the camera still recording: say it, clap, then stop. The clap and your voice let the editor line up the sound.

## What the footage has to be

Before you pack up, check this list. It is the footage checklist, not the kit checklist.

- [ ] Every prompt has an answer, each in its own take.
- [ ] Every take is landscape.
- [ ] Your face is in focus, level with the lens, and lit from the front or side.
- [ ] No window behind you.
- [ ] The clip-on microphone was on for everything you said.
- [ ] Each take has the date, camera name and take number said aloud, then a clap, at the start or the end.
- [ ] You have b-roll (the shots of your place and your work).
- [ ] You have thirty seconds of room tone in every place you filmed.

If one take is not right, film it again. Leave the first one on the card too. Nothing gets deleted.

## If something goes wrong

A dead battery, a full card, a broken part, a camera that will not turn on: stop. Do not open the camera, do not try to fix it, and do not change settings to get round it. Call or write to us. Your filming session moves; the camera does not go to a shop. We work out what to do together, and a spare may already be in the box.

If you drop or damage something, tell us, take photographs, and pack it back as usual. We record it as damage and nothing more.

## How it comes back

- **The cards stay put.** Leave them in the camera or in the card case. Do not copy, delete or wipe anything, and do not format a card, even if the camera offers to.
- **Everything goes in the box.** Camera, lens, microphone, cables, batteries, charger, stand, and the card case, in the same box it came in, with the kit checklist.
- **The return label is already inside.** Stick it over the old one, close the box and hand it to the carrier. Send us the tracking number.
- **An upload is optional.** If we give you a link, you can upload a second copy of your footage there. It is a backup. The cards in the box are the copy that counts.

Your footage comes back to you. Nobody wipes a card before you have it.

Questions at any point: call or write to us. You are never bothering us.
