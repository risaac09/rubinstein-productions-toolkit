# Remote Session Runbook

> **Status:** accepted 2026-10-16, with open parameters. The runbook for the filming phase of the camera-shipping model, where the facilitator guides the client over a call while the client films themselves. Written to the decisions Isaac made on Amendment 3, item 10. No client has used it yet; [Open parameters](#open-parameters) lists what is not set.

The [blueprint](../methodology/methodology-blueprint.md) calls this Phase 3: the camera is with the client, and the facilitator guides remotely. The [facilitation protocol](../methodology/facilitation-protocol.md) and the [session guide](../methodology/session-facilitation-guide.md) are written for a shared room. This runbook carries their consent loop and decompression over a call. It sits between [camera-shipping logistics](camera-shipping-logistics.md), which gets the kit to the client and back, and the [client shooting brief](client-shooting-brief.md), which tells the client what to film.

## What stays fixed

- **Calls are not recorded.** The camera in the client's hands records the footage. The call carries the facilitation, and nothing on it is kept except the facilitator's notes. Not recording keeps the call inside consent tier 1, the default in [consent evidence](consent-evidence.md), so no extra artifact is needed for it.
- **The facilitator writes the prompts.** Nobody else does.
- **No measurement during filming.** Filming is the Zero-Measurement Zone (see below).
- **Consent is spoken at the start and end of every call,** and the yes goes in the session record.

## Prompts

The facilitator writes the prompts from the [Core Question Bank](../methodology/session-facilitation-guide.md#core-question-bank) in the session guide, including its On-Camera Specific section, and shapes them to what the earlier phases surfaced about this client. The prompts are written before the kit ships, printed, and put in the box beside the shooting brief. The client answers them one take each, as the brief says.

The bank is the source and the guide is mirrored, so this runbook never edits it. A prompt that is not in the bank is allowed. A prompt that the facilitator would not ask in a room is not.

The number of prompts per box is not set (see [Open parameters](#open-parameters)).

## The setup call

This is step 7 of the [shipment lifecycle](camera-shipping-logistics.md#the-shipment-lifecycle), the last point where a replacement can still arrive before the first session. It runs 30 minutes with the receiving person, and the participant joins if they are not the same person. The client has the box open, the camera on the stand and the brief to hand. The times below are a plan for the 30 minutes, not limits.

| Minutes | Block | What the facilitator does |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Arrive and consent | Settling breath. Spoken consent check (below). Say that the call is not recorded. |
| 3 to 8 | Power | The client plugs in the camera and switches it on. Confirm the battery shows charge and the card shows space. Confirm the spare battery and the charger are in the box. |
| 8 to 18 | Test clip and playback | The client presses record, claps once, says the date and take number, and speaks for thirty seconds in their normal voice. They stop, play it back on the camera's screen, and tell you what they see and hear. Fix what is wrong, then record and play back once more. |
| 18 to 25 | Framing | Lens at eye level. Landscape. Light on the face, no window behind. Ask the client to move the camera, not the settings. Check that the face is in focus. |
| 25 to 28 | Sound check | The lav is on, about a hand's width below the chin. The client speaks and you listen on the call, then compare with the playback. Room tone: thirty seconds of quiet, with the room still. |
| 28 to 30 | Close | Agree the date and time of the first session call. Confirm the contact details. Spoken end-of-call consent check. |

Three rules for the call. **The settings stay as they left.** The camera was locked to the [capture standard](capture-standard.md) before shipping, and the setup call checks that it still is; it does not adjust it. If a setting looks wrong, the facilitator notes it. A fault in the kit follows the [damage and loss](camera-shipping-logistics.md#damage-and-loss) table. **Nothing from the test clip is kept for the project;** it stays on the card with the rest, since nothing is deleted. **The call ends with the client able to start alone.** If the client could not, a second setup call is booked before the first session.

## Session calls

One call per filming session, the length the facilitator and client agree. A session call is a Type 3 session in the session guide, and its special protocol applies: follow energy, not agenda.

**Start.** Settle, then the spoken consent check. Ask what is off limits today. Ask the calibration question from the guide (present, 1 to 10) and take two minutes of breath together if the answer is under 6. Agree which prompts the client will answer.

**During.** The client films each answer as its own take, with the clap and slate the brief describes. The facilitator watches the call, not the camera, and holds the room as the guide describes: slow, stay with the answer, let the pause run. Reconfirm consent at natural breaks, as the protocol says. The client can pass on any prompt or stop for any reason, and the facilitator says so again at the first break.

**End.** The spoken end-of-call consent check. The client turns the camera off before any emotional debrief, as the protocol requires: de-escalation happens person to person, not on camera. Then the five-minute decompression window from the protocol and guide, with no technical talk. No "did the clap work", no card questions, no scheduling. Technical notes go in a message afterward.

### The consent loop over a call

The protocol asks for permission to begin recording and a reaffirmation at natural breaks. On a call the facilitator does not record, so the loop asks about the client's own filming and about what happens to the footage, and it is spoken, not typed.

**Start of every call.** The facilitator says it in their own words, along these lines:

> "Before we start: you're filming, I'm not recording this call. Everything you film stays on your cards and comes back to you. Nothing is used without your approval. You can pass on a question or stop whenever you want. Are you okay to go ahead?"

**End of every call.**

> "Before we finish: is there anything you said or filmed today that you want to take back, or that you want me to treat carefully? Is it okay to keep what you filmed?"

**The record.** The facilitator notes the yes in the session record the same day: the date, the call (setup, session number or debrief), whether the start and end checks were given, the client's answer in their words, and anything they named as off limits or want taken back. A no, or a pause that was not a yes, is also recorded, and the facilitator does not proceed as if it were a yes. The record is the engagement's own, held with the signed scope and not in this repo.

If the client wants something taken back, nothing is deleted from the cards before the client has their footage; what happens to it is settled at the debrief. The facilitator tells the client that on the call.

## The Zero-Measurement Zone

Filming is the Zero-Measurement Zone, defined in the [session guide](../methodology/session-facilitation-guide.md#type-3-on-camera-conversation-production): no extractive data collection, follow energy and not agenda. Over a call, that means no survey forms sent, no scores asked for, no rating of how the client is doing, no coding of what is said while the sessions run, and no questions whose purpose is a data point. The calibration question at the start of a session is the guide's own grounding step, not a measure. The facilitator's own notes and the same-day consent record are not measurement.

## Debrief call

Within 48 hours of the last filming session, the facilitator holds a debrief call. The footage is still on the cards in the box or on its way back, so the debrief works from what the client remembers, not from review. The call opens and closes with the same spoken consent checks.

1. Ask how it was, in the client's words, before anything else. Reflect back what you noticed, as the protocol does after filming.
2. Ask what they want to be sure is in the piece, and what they want left out. Note the answers verbatim.
3. Ask whether any take should be treated carefully or taken back, and note the answer.
4. Confirm the return: the pack date, the tracking, and that the cards stay put, per the [shooting brief](client-shooting-brief.md#how-it-comes-back).
5. Close with the end-of-call consent check and the five-minute decompression window.

The debrief is a call, not a survey. Any assessment point that falls near it follows the instrument's own schedule, not this runbook.

## Open parameters

These are not decided. None is a statement of current practice.

- **Prompt count and review.** How many prompts go in a box, and whether the client sees them before the setup call.
- **Session length and count.** The blueprint says one to three filming sessions over one to two weeks; the length of each call is not set.
- **Debrief start point.** "Within 48 hours" is counted here from the last filming session. The source decision did not say from what.
- **Setup-call minute allocation.** The plan in the table is a first split of the 30 minutes.
- **Session record format.** The fields above are the minimum. The record's place and form belong to the engagement, not to this repo.
- **Missed calls and dropped connections.** What happens when a call fails mid-session is not set.
- **Languages and accessibility.** Calls in a language the facilitator does not share, and clients who cannot use video, are not covered.
