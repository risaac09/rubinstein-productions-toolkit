# The evaluation framework, in plain language

> **Canonical doc:** [`methodology/evaluation-framework.md`](evaluation-framework.md). That file is the source of truth, a snapshot of the vault canonical. This is the teaching layer for readers with no evaluation background.

## The problem it answers

Most program evaluation counts what is easy to count: sessions held, people reached, videos produced. Those numbers satisfy funders, and they say almost nothing about whether anything changed for the people involved. The framework refuses to choose between the two. It measures in both languages at once and calls the result the Bilingual Dashboard.

## The two languages

**Royal Metrics** are the counts institutions already trust. For Say Why they cover three areas:

- Outputs: sessions completed, participants finishing the full arc (the target is 80 percent or better retention), one video and one written narrative per participant, all camera equipment returned.
- Reach: total participants (10 to 15 in year one), organizations represented, geographic spread, how many participants put their video to public use.
- Financial accountability: cost per participant, equipment utilization, the value of participants' own time.

**Nomadic Indicators** track what the counts miss. Three clusters, each with its own method:

- Narrative coherence, measured before and after. An interviewer asks "tell me about your work" at intake and again 30 days after completion, then codes the answers for jargon, hedging, directness, and specificity. A related question: can the video carry the participant's story without the participant in the room?
- Relational quality, drawn from participant reflections and facilitator field notes. How fast did trust form. Were there moments where the participant said "that's it." Was consent ongoing rather than a form signed once. Did both sides leave more coherent, or did one side extract from the other?
- Emergent capacity, checked at 60 days and six months. Is the participant still using the footage, still filming, still speaking publicly? Did participants connect with each other? Did the shift ripple into their organization?

## How the data gets collected

Eight methods, each mapped to one or both languages: an intake interview, facilitator field notes, session recordings made with consent, output tracking, a reflection seven days after completion, a 60-day survey, a six-month check-in, and a comparison of the participant's intake language against their on-camera language.

## Who gets the results

Funders receive a quarterly report: the Royal summary plus two or three Nomadic narratives. Numbers for accountability, stories for truth. Each participant receives their own personal Bilingual Dashboard after the engagement, returned to them the way the footage is. Once a year the findings go to the field as a practice-based research publication.

## What the framework does not claim

It does not claim either language works alone. The canonical doc is explicit that both are necessary and neither is sufficient. The targets are intentions set per cohort, and no result has been claimed against them. The Nomadic Indicators are coded observations and structured interviews rather than standardized instruments, and the framework does not present them as such. It also does not claim the numbers prove transformation; that is the gap the second language exists to name.
