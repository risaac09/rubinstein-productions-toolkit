# The theory of change, in plain language

> **Canonical doc:** [`methodology/theory-of-change.md`](theory-of-change.md). That file is the source of truth, a snapshot of the vault canonical. This is the teaching layer for readers with no evaluation background.

## What a theory of change is

A theory of change is the standard document funders use to check whether a program's logic holds: what goes in, what happens, what comes out, and why anyone should expect the chain to connect. This one belongs to Say Why, the social impact initiative of Rubinstein Productions.

## The problem, stated plainly

People who work in nonprofits, public health, and community organizations often cannot say what they know in plain words. The canonical doc argues this is a structural condition rather than a personal failure. Jargon, hedging, and silence are rational responses to institutions that punish honest speech. The doc treats this as a public health problem: when communities cannot articulate their own conditions, they lose power over those conditions, and programs get funded on compliance language instead of truth.

## The intervention

Say Why runs a four-phase facilitation and film process: Container, Excavation, Performance, Translation. A facilitator builds relational safety, works with the participant through layers of institutional script, then ships a camera to the participant, who films themselves in their own space. Professional production turns the footage into a short video that can travel without the participant in the room. All footage returns to the participant. The doc calls this anti-extractive design and means it materially: the participant holds the camera, owns the raw material, and keeps the artifact.

## Why the chain should connect

The doc names one central mechanism, witnessing under conditions of relational safety, and grounds it in four bodies of work:

- Polyvagal co-regulation (Porges): a regulated facilitator settles the participant's nervous system, which opens access to autobiographical memory and honest speech.
- Self-authorship theory: participants move from performing for external validation toward speaking from internal authority, through practice rather than instruction.
- Anti-extractive design: agency is built into who holds the equipment and who owns the footage.
- Recursive inquiry: the facilitator asks "why" repeatedly, moving through institutional script and personal narrative toward embodied truth.

## What is expected to result

Short term, participants recognize the structural nature of their communication barriers, produce honest self-authored media, and gain camera literacy. Medium term, their organizations communicate more clearly, the artifacts travel to funders and communities, and peer networks form across cohorts. Long term, the doc aims at a sector-level shift from compliance language to clarity, and a narrower gap between communities and the institutions that serve them.

## Who it serves

Primary: mission-driven professionals in social impact, public health, and community-serving organizations. Secondary: community-based organizations whose public narrative lags the quality of their work, especially those serving communities that are spoken about rather than speaking for themselves. The pilot is Seattle metro, with remote delivery possible nationally.

## What the theory of change does not claim

It does not claim results. It is a logic model, a statement of expected causation, and the long-term impact column describes what the work aims at rather than what has been measured. It does not claim to be coaching, curriculum, or media training; the doc positions the work upstream of all three, at the condition that produces incoherent communication. The mechanism rests on cited theory and on the design of the process, and the paired evaluation framework exists because the chain still has to be checked against evidence.
