# Total Cost of Ownership

What does it cost to be alive right now. Not in dollars. In everything.

Every breath: oxygen crosses alveolar membrane into blood. Hemoglobin binds. Mitochondria oxidize glucose. ATP is produced. CO2 is waste. Heat is waste. The cell paid for that moment. You did not choose it. It happened before you could form the thought "breathe."

That is the base cost. Everything else stacks on top.

## The four directions

### North: Work
What gets spent. Labor, attention, metabolic energy, hours, money. The effort of maintenance. Jung called this the function of thinking: the ordering principle, the part that builds structure and bears load. In the Abhidhamma, this is cetanā (volition), the mental factor that organizes all other factors toward action. The cell equivalent: ATP hydrolysis. Energy released. Work done. Irreversible.

Every day brings the cost of maintaining a body, a shelter, a role, a set of relationships. The economic TCO lives here. So does the psychosocial: the energy it takes to hold a position in a social field. For some bodies this cost is higher. A Black body in a white institution spends ATP that a white body does not. Du Bois named this double consciousness. Fanon named it epidermalization. The cost is not metaphorical. It is metabolic. The sympathetic nervous system activates. Cortisol spends. The bill comes in inflammation, hypervigilance, shortened telomeres.

### East: Innocence
What arrives before interpretation. The pre-reflective. Sensation before concept. Merleau-Ponty called this the lived body: the hand that touches before the mind names "touching." In the Abhidhamma, this is phassa (contact) and vedanā (feeling-tone): pleasant, unpleasant, neutral. Every sense impression carries a tone before you know it. Light hits retina. Sound pressure deforms the tympanic membrane. Mechanoreceptors in skin fire. You feel before you think.

The cost here is what gets lost in translation. The moment sensation becomes concept, something is already gone. Husserl's Lebenswelt, the lifeworld, is the field of innocence: what was there before theory. Most frameworks begin after this loss. The TCO must include what it costs to stop sensing. To live in the head. To default to abstraction when the body is still speaking.

For a child this direction is open. For an adult it narrows. Butler's performativity names one mechanism: the body learns to perform its gender, its race, its class before it learns it is performing. The innocence is spent. The cost is not visible until someone asks: what did you feel before you knew what you were supposed to feel?

### South: Transition
Where things metabolize. The place of making-sense. Not clarity yet. The digestive phase. Jung called this the feeling function: valuation, not emotion. What matters. What is worth keeping. The Abhidhamma maps this with extraordinary precision: the 52 cetasikas (mental factors) that arise and pass in each moment of consciousness. Some are universal (contact, feeling, perception, attention, volition). Some are unwholesome (greed, hatred, delusion, conceit, envy). Some are beautiful (faith, mindfulness, moral shame, non-greed, non-hatred, equanimity). Every moment of consciousness is composed of a specific combination. Nothing is wasted. Everything is accounted for.

Transition costs the most when it is blocked. Critical theory names the mechanism: reification. The living process of making-sense gets frozen into a thing. Adorno's administered life. Marcuse's one-dimensional thought. The system produces subjects who cannot metabolize their own experience because the categories are pre-given. You feel rage but the form is "dissatisfaction with service." You feel grief but the form is "underperformance."

The somatic cost: dorsal vagal shutdown. The body stops processing. Food sits undigested. Emotion sits unmetabolized. The vagus nerve goes quiet. This is the total cost of cruelty operating at the level of the gut.

### West: Clarity
What remains after processing. Not knowledge. Seeing. The Abhidhamma locates this in paññā (wisdom): direct comprehension of the three marks. Impermanence is not a concept. It is what you see when you look at anything closely enough. Heidegger's Lichtung, the clearing: not an answer but an opening in which things can show themselves.

The cost of clarity is that it cannot be stored. It is not an asset. You do not own it. It arises in a moment of consciousness and passes. The next moment may be confusion. The economic frame cannot hold this because it assumes accumulation. The Buddhist frame cannot hold accumulation because it assumes impermanence. The tension between them is not a problem to solve. It is the oscillation itself.

Race and gender theory adds: clarity is not evenly distributed. Ahmed's work on affective economies shows how certain bodies are positioned as the ones who feel while others are positioned as the ones who know. Crenshaw's intersectional reading sharpens the point: the cost of clarity for a body marked as "emotional" is that clarity will be read as emotion. The cost of clarity for a body marked as "rational" is that clarity will require dismantling the apparatus of rationality first.

## The cost per breath

| Layer | What gets spent | Lens |
|---|---|---|
| Cellular | O2 in, CO2 out, ATP cycled, heat lost | Biochemistry |
| Sensory | Five channels firing, mechanoreceptors, proprioception, interoception | Phenomenology (Merleau-Ponty) |
| Nervous system | Vagal tone, sympathetic/parasympathetic balance, polyvagal state | Polyvagal theory (Porges) |
| Mental factors | Contact, feeling-tone, perception, volition, attention (minimum) | Abhidhamma |
| Psychological | Attention allocated, emotion regulated, defense maintained | Jung, object relations |
| Social | Position held, performance maintained, code-switching cost | Butler, Du Bois, Fanon |
| Economic | Time spent, opportunity forgone, labor extracted | Classical TCO |
| Structural | Which bodies absorb which costs by default | Critical theory, Ahmed, Crenshaw |

Every row is happening simultaneously. In every breath. The total cost is the stack.

## Say Why as one oscillation

Say Why was never a series. It was a single gesture that keeps trying to name itself. The gesture: I will sit with you while you say the thing you have not said. That is all of it. That is the listening (Gate 13) and the contribution (Gate 8). That is North and East and South and West happening at the same time in one breath.

The oscillation is: the cost of being alive includes the cost of being heard. And the cost of being heard includes the cost of someone choosing to listen. Both costs are real. Both costs are metabolic. Both costs are paid per breath.

Total cost of ownership is not a framework to be applied. It is a description of what is already happening. Every millisecond. Whether or not anyone is paying attention.
