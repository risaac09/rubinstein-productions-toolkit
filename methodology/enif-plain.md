# The measurement framework (ENIF), in plain language

> **Canonical doc:** [`methodology/measurement-framework.md`](measurement-framework.md). That file is the source of truth, a snapshot of the vault canonical and the April 2026 consolidation of the original 780-line ENIF. This is the teaching layer for readers with no evaluation background.

## What it is

This is the client-work measurement system for Rubinstein Productions, the practice-level counterpart to the grant-facing Say Why evaluation framework. Most agencies pick a side: art, which goes unmeasured, or ROI, which goes soulless. This framework tracks every engagement in two registers at once and reports them together as the Bilingual Dashboard.

## Register 1: institutional metrics

The language a CFO or board already trusts. Four families:

- Financial performance: referral rates after an engagement, repeat business, time from inquiry to signed contract, how often clients use what was delivered.
- Brand and communication: pre and post scores for how clearly stakeholders can state the mission, alignment between internal culture and external messaging, engagement on shared content.
- Developmental ROI: how much faster the team reaches strategic consensus, willingness to be vulnerable on record, the narrowing gap between the official narrative and lived experience.
- Satisfaction: seven Likert questions plus a Net Promoter Score, collected at five points from pre-engagement to three months out.

## Register 2: relational indicators

What emerged that nobody predicted. The primary metric is consistency mapping: whether the organization's narrative can hold contradictory experience without flattening it. Diverse stakeholders rate statements like "I can locate myself in this story without erasing complexity" on a ten-point scale, and high variance between leadership, staff, and beneficiaries counts as a failure at the intersections. Around that core sit four other lenses: conversational texture (pauses, self-corrections, emergent metaphors, pronoun shifts), process indicators (moments where rigid thinking breaks open, and insights that arrive from outside the original scope), a four-layer fear inventory that follows a presenting worry down to its root and out to a changed relationship with it, and somatic markers, the observable body signals of opening or closing during a session.

## How it runs

Session transcripts get coded by hand against a six-domain guide, generously on the first pass. The quantitative side lives in Airtable across five tables, with a weekly five-minute update, a monthly half hour, and deeper quarterly and annual reviews. On the last Friday of each month the practitioner runs a two to three hour synthesis ritual: immerse in the data, look for patterns across clients, analyze consistency scores, and write a memo.

## What the client sees

The registers are internal analytical categories, never client-facing language. Tier 2 and above engagements receive the Bilingual Dashboard, a single report pairing each institutional metric with its relational counterpart. Two impact conversations follow delivery, at two weeks and three months, after a deliberate no-contact window that lets the work settle.

## The safeguards

Measurement of this depth could turn extractive, so the framework builds in consent tiers (internal learning by default, anonymized aggregates by opt-in, identified case studies only with explicit permission and compensation), data sovereignty (clients can pull their raw data, edit reflections, veto public sharing, and withdraw from case studies retroactively), and reciprocity, including anonymized pattern memos back to clients. Contracts state the purpose openly: the measurement builds the methodology while it assesses the engagement.

## What the framework does not claim

The target benchmarks are labeled starting points to adjust with experience, and no result has been claimed against them. The consolidation cut the original ENIF's aspirational material on AI analysis, agent-based modeling, and certification, to be revisited at 25 or more client engagements, so none of that is claimed as current practice. The scoring scales are the practice's own instruments rather than validated psychometrics, and the framework presents its relational indicators as things that matter precisely because they resist standardization.
